01 · What this is

What you're setting up.

The LLM Council fixes the one-confident-answer trap with structure: Claude frames the real decision and pulls relevant context, 5 advisors answer in parallel, the answers get relabeled Response A to E and all 5 review the set blind, then a chairman reads everything and gives you one clear verdict. Not "it depends."

It's for business owners making calls where being wrong is expensive: pricing, positioning, whether to take the deal, hire or automate.

You need

  • Claude: web app, desktop app, or Claude Code. Any of them.
  • The repo: github.com/aiwithremy/claude-skills-llm-council
  • 30 seconds. No code.
Heads up: The Council is only as sharp as what you feed it. "Should I raise my rates?" gets generic advice. Give it real numbers, a genuine fork, and run it BEFORE the move. After you've committed, it's just expensive hindsight. Skip it for questions with one correct answer, creation tasks, summaries, or anything low-stakes.

04

Know the 5 advisors

Thinking lenses, not job titles, each deliberately one-sided. The Contrarian assumes your idea has a fatal flaw and hunts for it. The First Principles Thinker asks what you're actually trying to solve and rebuilds from zero. The Expansionist hunts the upside everyone else is missing. The Outsider has zero context about you and catches the curse of knowledge. The Executor only cares what you do Monday morning.

05

Read the verdict like a map

The chairman's verdict comes back in five parts: where the council agrees (high-confidence, move), where it clashes (the real decision lives there, slow down and dig), blind spots caught in peer review, one recommendation, and the one thing to do first. Most people skim to the recommendation and skip the clash section. That's the part that pays.

06

Push back on it

The verdict isn't final. Ask for a second pass that argues against the first, or zoom in on the advisor whose angle worried you.

pressure-test that recommendation
say more about what the Contrarian flagged

07

Feed it a war room, not a one-liner

Drop in real numbers, your constraints, what you've tried and what's at stake. Bring forks (raise or hold, hire or automate), not lookups. For copy review, paste the actual draft so the Outsider can catch where it confuses people. Go in wanting the answer you don't want. Do the one first step, then come back and council your next fork.

03 · Copy and paste

Your prompts.

PROMPTRaise your prices or hold

council this: I run a service business at about $9k/month. I charge $1,500/mo per client and my last two prospects pushed back on price, but I'm also at capacity and turning down work. Do I raise to $2,000 for new clients and grandfather current ones, raise across the board and risk churn, or hold and fix capacity with systems first?

PROMPTHire your first person vs automate

council this: I'm maxed on hours and turning down work. Option A: hire a part-time contractor at $1,800/month, eats most of my profit for months and I've never managed anyone. Option B: build automation (Claude Code + n8n) to take the repetitive work off me. Hire now, automate first, or raise prices to thin the workload?
