01 · What this is

What you're setting up.

Readers have seen enough AI text to spot it. One giveaway is fine. Three in one email and the reader stops trusting the rest, including your price.

Most of these are habits AI models fall into: long dashes, "it's not just X, it's Y", lists of three, words like "delve". Delete them and the email reads like a person wrote it, because a person checked it.

You need

  • The email, post or proposal you're about to send
  • Any Claude plan for the check prompts
  • 2 minutes per email
Heads up: This list catches patterns, not facts. A clean-sounding email can still have a wrong price or name. Read it for facts after you fix the style.

The checklist

Delete this. Write that.

01

1. Long dashes mid-sentence

Delete the long dash (the one between two words, like this example: fast—cheap). Use a period or a comma. "We ship fast. Most orders land in 2 days."

02

2. "It's not just X, it's Y"

Say the real thing. Not "It's not just a tool, it's a partner" but "It answers your DMs at night."

03

3. Three things in a row

Lists of three for rhythm ("fast, simple and powerful") sound written by a machine. Two is fine. One real detail is better.

04

4. "Delve", "seamless", "leverage"

Swap for plain words: "look at", "smooth", "use". Same for "robust", "pivotal", "showcase" and "foster".

05

5. "Let's dive in"

Delete the warm-up. Start with the point: "Here's the quote for the April job."

06

6. "Great question!"

Answer the question. Praise before an answer reads as filler, and clients notice.

07

7. "Honestly?" "Here's the thing"

Fake-candid openers. Just say it: "The deadline won't work. Can we do the 14th?"

08

8. "At its core"

Write the actual claim. "At its core, our service is about trust" becomes "We answer within 2 hours."

09

9. "Serves as", "boasts"

Use "is" and "has". "The office boasts parking" becomes "The office has parking."

10

10. "In order to", "due to the fact that"

Use "to" and "because". Shorter, and it sounds like you talking.

PROMPT2. Fix it in my voice

Rewrite this email with those giveaways removed. Keep my words where they're fine, keep it the same length or shorter, and don't add anything I didn't say.

[paste email]

PROMPT3. Stop them at the source

Add this to your project instructions:
Never use long dashes. Never write "it's not just X, it's Y". Don't list three things for rhythm. Never use: delve, seamless, leverage, robust, pivotal, showcase, foster. No warm-up openers, no praise before answers. Use "is" and "has", not "serves as" or "boasts".
